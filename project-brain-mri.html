Brain MRI Surface Registration & Anatomical Alignment

Non-rigid deep learning registration between predicted 3D cortical meshes and ground-truth MRI surface representations, preserving topological integrity across complex sulcal folds. Supported by work at the Xu Lab, Carnegie Mellon University.

Code: GitHub Repository Dataset: IXI Brain MRI Benchmark (560+ subjects) Preprocessed Artifact: Public IXI002 Subject FreeSurfer Bundle Inspection: Freeview .mgz / .obj

§ 1. Problem Formulation

Human cortical surfaces possess intricate, highly variable geometric folds (sulci and gyri). Volumetric voxel-based registration algorithms (such as standard affine transforms or SyN) operate on uniform 3D grid volumes, inevitably blurring thin cortical gray-matter ribbons (1.5mm–3mm thickness) and collapsing opposing sulcal banks into singular averaged voxels.

§ 2. Motivation & Research Goal

Registering subjects directly in continuous 2D surface manifold space (parameterized conformally onto spherical topologies) circumvents volumetric partial volume effects. However, classical spherical registration tools (such as FreeSurfer's iterative spherical morphing) require 6 to 10 hours of CPU computation per brain scan. We investigated deep learning surface deformation networks that achieve sub-second non-rigid mesh alignment while enforcing topological guarantees (zero inverted mesh faces).

§ 3. Method & Surface Deformation Framework

The pipeline ingests raw volumetric T1-weighted MRI scans, extracts cortical boundary meshes, parameterizes them onto a regularized sphere, and trains a deformation field network guided by composite surface losses.

Registration Pipeline Architecture: Raw T1 MRI ➔ FreeSurfer recon-all ➔ Cortical Boundary Extraction (.mgz/.obj) ➔ Spherical Parameterization ➔ Deep Surface Deformation Network ➔ Topological Consistency Loss ➔ Registered Cortical Mesh

Loss Formulation: The loss function balances anatomical alignment against mesh regularity:

ℒ_total = ℒ_Chamfer(S_deformed, S_target) + λ_1 · ℒ_Normal_Consistency + λ_2 · ℒ_Laplacian_Distortion

§ 4. Experimental Setup

Dataset: IXI Benchmark dataset (Information eXtraction from Images), containing T1-weighted scans acquired at 1.5T and 3.0T across multiple clinical hospitals (Guys, Hammersmith, IOP). 50 subjects were selected and preprocessed.

Compute & Tools: FreeSurfer 7.4.1 for ground-truth volumetric reconstruction, PyTorch 3D for mesh rendering and differential surface losses, running on Linux computing clusters.

Hyperparameters: Chamfer weight = 1.0, Normal consistency weight λ_1 = 0.1, Laplacian smoothness λ_2 = 0.05, Adam optimizer with lr = 1e-4, batch size = 2 meshes (due to 150k vertex counts), 100 training epochs.

§ 7. Scientific Limitations & Threats to Validity

Scientific Limitations 1. Upstream Dependency: Generating the initial spherical parameterization still requires FreeSurfer's volumetric reconstruction; while inference is instantaneous, data preprocessing is computationally demanding.
2. Healthy Cohort Assumption: Evaluated on healthy adult brains from IXI; performance on severe structural deformations (e.g., large glioblastomas or hydrocephalus) has not been demonstrated.

Future Work: Building an end-to-end direct voxel-to-mesh deformation network that infers cortical surfaces in a single forward pass, bypassing the multi-hour FreeSurfer preprocessing requirement.
