RESEARCH CASE STUDY // CLINICAL DEPLOYMENT & DIAGNOSTIC AI

BrainScan AI: Multi-Class MRI Diagnostic Interface

Interactive clinical web interface powered by the two-stage transfer learning model accepted at ICMLDE 2025, providing instantaneous inference, confidence distributions, and thresholded clinical reporting across 4 brain MRI categories.

Code: GitHub Repository ↗ Weights: Checkpoints in repository Accuracy: 97.88% Multi-Class Test Conference: ICMLDE 2025

§ 1. Problem Formulation

Brain tumor diagnosis from multi-sequence MRI requires accurate differentiation between Glioma, Meningioma, Pituitary tumor, and normal brain scans. Because scanner hardware, magnetic field strengths, and acquisition protocols vary across clinical imaging centers, single-stage transfer learning models easily overfit to scanner-specific artifacts and suffer representation collapse when fine-tuned on limited patient cohorts.

§ 2. Motivation & Two-Stage Training Protocol

Directly unfreezing deep convolutional weights on small medical datasets causes catastrophic forgetting of generalized low-level filters (edges, textures). We formulated a two-stage protocol: Stage 1 trains specialized dense classification heads and dropout regularizers while freezing the convolutional feature extractor; Stage 2 unfreezes the upper convolutional blocks with a conservative learning rate (1e-5) for subtle clinical feature adaptation.

§ 3. Architecture & Clinical Pipeline

The system pairs a modified VGG16 backbone with dense regularized classification heads and an interactive Streamlit UI.

Diagnostic Workflow: Uploaded MRI Scan ➔ Image Resizing & Normalization ➔ VGG16 Feature Extractor ➔ Stage 1 Dense Head ➔ Stage 2 Block Unfreezing (lr=1e-5) ➔ 4-Class Softmax Output (97.88%)

§ 4. Experimental Setup

Dataset: Benchmark collection of 3,264 multi-sequence T1, T2, and FLAIR brain MRI scans spanning Glioma, Meningioma, Pituitary, and Healthy control scans.

Compute & Stack: Google Colab GPU environment (NVIDIA T4), PyTorch, Torchvision, Scikit-learn, OpenCV, Streamlit.

Hyperparameters: Stage 1: Adam optimizer with lr = 1e-3, 15 epochs; Stage 2: lr = 1e-5, 20 epochs, batch size = 32, categorical cross-entropy loss, data augmentation with rotation and zoom.

§ 7. Scientific Limitations & Threats to Validity

Scientific Limitations 1. Slice-Level Context: The model evaluates 2D MRI slices without full 3D volumetric context, meaning slice selection quality impacts classification confidence.
2. Prospective Validation: Performance is verified on retrospective public datasets; prospective validation in active clinical imaging suites with varied pulse sequence timing is still required.

Future Work: Upgrading the backend to 3D volumetric segmentation networks (3D Swin UNETR) and embedding Grad-CAM attention visualizations directly into the UI report.
